For the grown-up

This is called making a ten. Counting on past 30 is hard because the ones digit wraps back to 0 and the tens digit goes up by one at the same moment. Here the child first fills the frame (28 needs 2 to become 30), then adds the leftover (30 + 3 = 33). When the number to add has a ten in it, as in 10 + 18, the whole ten goes first. Only the tens digit changes, then the ones are added.

Ask: "How many more to the next ten?" before pressing Next.

About these math visualizations

Little Math Lab is a free interactive math visualizer for 1st grade and 2nd grade. Each subject can be shown as a car on a road, hops on a number line, a row of beads, ten frames or a train, so a child can pick the picture that makes sense to them.

Adding past a ten

Sums like 28 + 5 are hard because the ones digit wraps around and the tens digit changes at the same moment. The make-a-ten strategy fills up to the next ten first (28 + 2 = 30), then adds what is left (30 + 3 = 33). Sums with a whole ten inside, like 10 + 18, add the ten first.

Subtracting across a ten

For 32 − 5, take away just enough to land on the ten (32 − 2 = 30), then take away the rest (30 − 3 = 27). When there are not enough ones, a ten is opened into 10 ones.

Counting by tens

Counting 14, 24, 34, 44 shows that adding ten only changes the tens digit. The ones stay the same.

Tens and ones (place value)

A number like 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones. Full ten frames or full train cars are tens, and the loose counters are ones.
